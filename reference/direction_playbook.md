# Direction Selection & Validation Playbook

> Compiled by parallel deep reading from 1,997 real founder interviews on "idea origins, context, validation, and direction selection"
> General ranking: cross-shard occurrence frequency = generality score

---

## 1. Top 10 Idea Sources (ranked by generality)

| # | Source | Generality | Core Logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Scratch Your Own Itch** | 8/8 | You keep getting stuck on the same problem and can't find a good solution. Build it for yourself first, then for others like you. |
| 2 | **Industry Experience / Workplace Insight** | 8/8 | You've been in an industry for years; productize a customer pain or outdated process you've seen over and over. |
| 3 | **Spot a Market Gap / Someone Else's Unmet Need** | 8/8 | The product you want doesn't exist, existing solutions are poor, or a segment of people is being ignored. |
| 4 | **Platform Opportunity / New Trend Window** | 7/8 | New platform / new API / new technology is just emerging, supply is scarce — get in first. |
| 5 | **Copy a Proven Model / Cross-Domain Transplant** | 7/8 | Move a business model proven in Market A to an underserved geography or niche segment. |
| 6 | **Pulled by Requests from Customers / People Around You** | 6/8 | Demand precedes the company — people keep asking "Can you sell this / can you do this for me?" |
| 7 | **Productize an Internal Tool** | 5/8 | You built the tool to run your main business; peers realize they need it too, so you spin it out as a product. |
| 8 | **Monetize a Hobby / Skill** | 5/8 | Directly productize a skill you've loved for years or already master. |
| 9 | **Personal Mission / Life Event** | 4/8 | You've experienced illness / hardship / a social problem firsthand; turn the healing process or solution into a product. |
| 10 | **Accidental Discovery / Demand Finds You** | 3/8 | You were doing something else; an accident or someone's request reveals a larger market. |

**Meta-law**: The top three (scratch your own itch, industry experience, spot a gap) all point to the same thing — **start where you already understand, already hurt, already see**, not invent a "sexy idea" out of thin air. Ideas from thin air lack real details, and are the most likely to result in a product no one wants.

## 2. Top 9 Demand Validation Methods (ranked by generality)

| # | Method | Generality | One-Line Summary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Get Paid / Get First Paying Customer** | 8/8 | Payment is the only hard signal; beats all verbal "I like it". |
| 2 | **Landing Page / Email List for Intent Collection** | 7/8 | Subscriber volume proves there's a real audience. |
| 3 | **MVP + Post to Community / Social to Gauge Reaction** | 7/8 | Look for real behavior (shares, questions, downloads), not likes. |
| 4 | **One-on-One Interviews with Target Customers** | 7/8 | Ask about pain points, not "what do you think of this idea". |
| 5 | **Pre-Sale / Crowdfunding (Kickstarter etc.)** | 6/8 | Real bookings with real money = strongest validation + seed capital. |
| 6 | **Dogfooding (Use It Yourself First)** | 5/8 | If you'd keep using it and pay for it, that's initial validation. |
| 7 | **Keyword / Search Volume Validation** | 5/8 | Confirm how many people are searching for the same problem. |
| 8 | **Free Version / MVP to Users First** | 5/8 | Judge from retention, feedback, and organic growth. |
| 9 | **Low-Cost Prototype + Test with People You Know** | 4/8 | Build something sellable at minimum cost; see if anyone buys. |

**Meta-law**: All high-generality methods point to the same sentence — **validate with real payment behavior, not ask "what do you think"**. Politeness isn't demand; verbal interest isn't willingness to pay.

## 3. Counterintuitive Lessons on Direction Selection

| # | Lesson | Generality |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Validate paid / business model before building** | 8/8 | |
| 2 | **Don't wait for perfection; ship a bad version first** | 7/8 | |
| 3 | **Niche down / focus; don't serve everyone** | 7/8 | |
| 4 | **Find the audience first; don't invent the product first** | 6/8 | |
| 5 | **Don't chase shiny objects / go for money alone** | 5/8 | |
| 6 | **High competition is a good signal (it means the market exists)** | 5/8 | |
| 7 | **A boring niche business is often more profitable than a sexy big-category play** | 4/8 | |
| 8 | **Timing / platform opportunity sometimes determines success more than the product itself** | 4/8 | |
| 9 | **Pick a direction you'll use every day and genuinely love** | 4/8 | |
| 10 | **Direction can change; pivot after validation** | 4/8 | |

**Three most important judgment sentences (the skill's "direction filter formula")**:
1. **Why me? Why now?** — Success depends on your unique insight/channel/trust, not an idea anyone can copy; catching the new platform/technology window amplifies the effect.
2. **Paid commitment before development** — get the first payment (or at least paid intent) before you invest. Otherwise, don't touch it.
3. **Narrow and deep > broad and shallow** — shrink to one audience, one pain point; easier to win than "selling everything".
